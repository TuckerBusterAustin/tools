How to copy data from one website to another automatically

A practical guide for freelancers who keep retyping the same details into web forms.

Plain note: Ferrywise is not built. It is a planned browser extension, so it cannot be compared with anything on features yet. Nothing here is a feature claim about it.

1. Manual copy-paste habits

Most freelancers start here: open both sites, copy a value, switch tabs, paste. It needs no setup and works on every site.

  • Best for: one-off jobs and sites you use rarely.
  • Weakness: slow, and easy to paste the wrong field or skip one.
  • Tip: keep a plain-text snippet file for blocks you paste often, such as your address or invoice terms.

2. Browser autofill

Most browsers can store names, addresses and similar details and fill them into forms for you.

  • Best for: standard contact and address fields.
  • Weakness: it only fills fields the browser recognises. It does not move data you just read on one site into another site.
  • Tip: review what is stored, and avoid saving anything sensitive on shared computers.

3. Free macro tools, in general terms

Free macro recorders and automation add-ons can record clicks and typing, then replay them. They suit repeated steps that always look the same.

  • Best for: a fixed routine you run many times a week.
  • Weakness: recordings can break when a site changes its layout, and some take time to learn.
  • We make no claims here about any named product's price or features. Any such figure would have to be marked "to verify, accessed on <date>" after the owner's own check.

4. When to use which

  • Rare, one-off task: copy-paste.
  • Standard personal or business details: browser autofill.
  • Same multi-step routine, often: a free macro tool, and expect some upkeep.
  • Data that must move from a page you read into a form elsewhere, repeatedly: this is the gap we are testing interest in.
