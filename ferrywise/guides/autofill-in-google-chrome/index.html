How to set up autofill in Google Chrome (and where it stops)

Chrome's built-in autofill can fill your name, address, phone, email, saved cards and passwords into web forms. This guide covers how to set it up, edit it, clear it and fix it when it doesn't appear. It ends with what autofill isn't designed to do.

Menu labels change a little between Chrome versions and between desktop and mobile. If a name below doesn't match exactly, look for the closest one.

Where Chrome's autofill settings are

On desktop (Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS):

1. Click the ⋮ menu in the top right.

2. Choose Settings.

3. Click Autofill and passwords in the left sidebar. In older versions it's just Autofill.

Or type this in the address bar:

`

chrome://settings/autofill

`

You'll see three main sections: Google Password Manager, Payment methods and Addresses and more.

On Android and iPhone: tap ⋮ (or … on iOS) > Settings. Then find Addresses and more, Payment methods and Password Manager in the list.

Set up addresses and contact details

1. Open Addresses and more.

2. Turn on Save and fill addresses.

3. Click Add and enter your name, organisation, street address, phone and email.

4. Save it.

Next time you click a name or address field, Chrome offers your saved profile in a dropdown. Pick it, and Chrome fills the matching fields on the form.

You can save more than one address, for example Home and Office. Chrome lists them all in the dropdown.

Set up payment methods

1. Open Payment methods.

2. Turn on Save and fill payment methods.

3. Click Add to enter a card, or let Chrome offer to save a card the next time you pay online.

Depending on your settings and device, Chrome may ask you to verify yourself (for example with your device's screen lock) before it fills a card. Leave that on if you share the computer.

Set up passwords

1. Open Google Password Manager.

2. Make sure Offer to save passwords is on (in settings).

3. Optionally turn on Sign in automatically so Chrome signs you in with saved details on sites where you've done it before.

If you're signed in to Chrome with sync turned on, your addresses, cards and passwords are available on your other devices too.

Edit or delete one entry

  • Desktop: in the relevant section, click ⋮ next to an entry, then Edit or Delete.
  • Mobile: tap the entry to edit it. On some versions you can swipe it to delete.

Clear old suggestions

Chrome also remembers things you typed into ordinary text fields (form history). To clear them all:

1. Open chrome://settings/clearBrowserData.

2. Pick a time range.

3. Tick Autofill form data (on the Advanced tab if you don't see it), and untick anything you want to keep.

4. Click Delete data.

To remove a single wrong suggestion, highlight it in the dropdown with the arrow keys and press Shift+Delete (on Mac, Shift+Fn+Delete).

When autofill doesn't show up

  • The toggle is off. Check that Save and fill addresses or Save and fill payment methods is on.
  • The site turns it off. Some sites mark fields with autocomplete="off" or use custom-built inputs, so Chrome doesn't recognise them.
  • Field names are unusual. Chrome guesses what each field is for. A field labelled "Ref #" or "Billing contact (AP)" won't match anything in your profile.
  • Incognito windows don't save new entries.
  • Your workplace manages Chrome. On company machines an administrator may have disabled autofill. Look for "Managed by your organization" in the menu.
  • An extension conflicts. A password manager or form extension may replace Chrome's dropdown with its own. Disable extensions one by one to check.

Where built-in autofill stops

Chrome's autofill is designed for personal, standard fields: who you are, where you live, how you pay and how you log in. Freelancers and small teams often hit its limits with work forms:

  • Custom fields. You can't add your own fields, such as a VAT number, a client's PO number, a project code or a standard job description. Chrome only fills the field types it knows.
  • Many clients, not one person. Your address profiles describe you. If you type a different client's details into the same supplier portal every week, autofill can't hold a "client record" and fill it as one set.
  • Copying between sites. Autofill can't read a record on one site, such as a CRM or an order email, and enter it into a form on another site.
  • Dropdowns, checkboxes and steps. It won't tick the same three boxes, choose the same dropdown option or click through a multi-page form for you.
  • Repeated runs. There's no "do this form again with these values" button.

For these jobs people usually use one of three things: a text-expander tool for snippets, the Recorder in Chrome DevTools for replaying fixed sequences (powerful, but aimed at developers), or a form-filling or macro extension.

Summary

  • Settings live at chrome://settings/autofill: passwords, payment methods, and addresses and more.
  • Turn on each Save and fill toggle, add your details, and edit or delete entries from the ⋮ menu.
  • Clear stale suggestions with Autofill form data or Shift+Delete.
  • Built-in autofill fills your personal details well. Custom business fields, multiple client records and copying data between sites need a different tool.
